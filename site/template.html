What does the yield cost?

A covered-call fund pays a high distribution by selling away part of the upside of the shares it holds. This page measures what was given up: each fund's total return beside the index fund that holds the same shares, and the options the fund had actually written on its last report date.

The whole category

Returns are for the three and twelve months ending at each fund's own report date, so rows with different dates are not directly comparable. "Calls written" is the face value of the written calls at their strike, as a share of net assets. A dash means the filings do not give the figure.

What this page does not show

  • No distribution yield. The filings used here do not carry distributions per share, and a current yield needs daily prices.
  • Nothing recent. Funds report monthly, but the SEC makes only the report for the last month of each fiscal quarter public, about two months later. Each fund's figures are as of its own report date.
  • US-registered funds only. European (UCITS) funds do not file this form.
  • No strike for funds that use notes. Where the call exposure sits inside equity-linked notes, the filing names the note, its issuer and its size, not the option terms.
  • Strikes are checked against the holding's name. For index options the strike field of the SEC data set can be cut short; where the name of the holding states a different strike, the name is used and the table says so.
  • One share class per fund. For funds with several classes the first class identifier is used.
Source: US Securities and Exchange Commission, Form N-PORT data sets, a work of the US government in the public domain. Figures are as filed by the funds and are not audited or corrected here.
